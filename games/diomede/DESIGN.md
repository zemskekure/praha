# Diomede: design

Two islands 3.8 km apart with the International Date Line between them: Big Diomede lives in tomorrow, Little
Diomede in yesterday. The islands are a metaphor. The game is about two people who feel they have always known each
other, and go back in time to find out why.

## The story

**Honza and Martina** choose the Diomede Islands for their first trip together because of the 21 hours between
them: here, they think, they can have more time together than anywhere else.

> „Proč jsme jeli sem? Vždyť je tu strašná zima.“

They feel an unusually strong bond, as if they had known each other forever. Neither knows where it came from. The
only way to find out is to go back in time: something in the past must have made it.

**The origin.** Long ago, before the border closed the strait, two young people from opposite islands met on the
winter ice, when the islanders still walked across to visit each other. They promised to meet again. Then the line
closed, and they never could. Their promise was left unfinished, waiting in the ice.

**The loop.** Honza and Martina do not just find that meeting. They cause it. Her fire on the cliff guides the
girl across the ice in the dark; the whippet leads the boy to her. The bond they feel is the promise they helped
make, and they came back because they already felt it.

**The ending is bittersweet and warm.** The old pair never meet again; the line keeps them apart for the rest of
their lives. But the promise is kept, through Honza and Martina, who return to the present together. The last scene
is the first: the hut, the fire, the cold. Now they know why.

## Decisions

- **You play Honza.** Martina is real, but for most of the game she is a day ahead of you on the other island: a
  light across the water, a voice at the date line. In the last chapter you play her, once, for the walk on the ice.
- **Time travel is real.** Rowing east across the date line takes you back. Each crossing goes one layer deeper.
- **Bittersweet and warm.** No villains, no fail state. Loss belongs to the past; warmth belongs to the two of them.

## Principles

1. **Every mechanic means two things.** Rowing is the effort to reach someone. The date line is what separates.
   The fire is warmth made together. The clocks are the time you share. A feature with only one meaning is cut.
2. **Time is the only resource.** When Honza and Martina are close, time slows: the clocks crawl. Apart, it runs.
   Holding T spends time, and the player should feel what they are spending.
3. **The past is answered by the present.** Traces of their future selves are already in the past: footprints that
   belong to no one, a fire already burning, a note in their own handwriting.
4. **Notice, don't solve.** No combat, no inventory puzzles. Progress comes from attention: where the light falls,
   what the dog stares at, what is different in this yesterday.
5. **The dog doesn't care about date lines.** The whippet moves freely between times. It finds things, waits where
   things matter, and in the oldest layer there is a dog that looks exactly like it.
6. **Small world, deep time.** The map never grows. The same two islands change across eras, and the player learns
   one place well enough to feel every change.

## How they talk

Across 21 hours, only light reaches: the fire on the cliff, the lantern, the lamps on the poles, signalled at night.
The only place where both times touch is the date line, so they meet on the water, in the boat.

## Layers of time

| Layer | When | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 · Arrival | now | Power poles, the hut, the pier, the rowing boat. Martina on Big Diomede, a day ahead. |
| 1 · Last year | a year back | Same places, another season. Their own traces start to appear. |
| 2 · Before the poles | decades back | No power line, an older hut, skin boats on the beach. |
| 3 · The ice | the old winter | The strait frozen. The border not yet closed. The meeting. |

## Build order

1. **Martina and time.** Martina on Big Diomede as a light you can see at night; the clocks slow when you are near
   each other (at the date line); the slow-time feeling made visible (light, sound, the world easing).
2. **Crossing back.** Rowing east across the line moves the world one layer into the past: props, light and season
   change per layer. Start with layer 1.
3. **Traces.** Footprints, a fire, a note: the first sign that they have been here before.
4. **The ice.** Layer 3: the frozen strait, walking across, the fire guiding her, the dog leading him.
5. **Words.** Short lines between them, in Czech, at the moments that matter. Nothing more than needed.
